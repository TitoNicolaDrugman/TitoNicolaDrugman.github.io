Tito Nicola Drugman (fl. present) is an Italian computer engineer, indefinitely enrolled master's student and self-described "guy who'd rather spend three days configuring a server than pay for a ten-euro-a-month subscription".[2] He is best known for stuffing transformers into microcontrollers too small to hold opinions of their own and for an encyclopedia article that, inexplicably, exists.[3]

Raised in Milan, he went on to be educated across three universities for his bachelor's, one university (but two degrees away) for his master's and a Python library for everything else. He is not to be confused with a vodka, a Yugoslav statesman, a Latin jazz percussionist or the recurring temptation to comment code with "don't touch it, it works".[citation needed]

Biography [edit | edit source]

Early years and dual-boot

Accredited sources place his birth on 31 March 2002 — Easter Sunday that year — with at least one family account mentioning a rainbow that appeared in the sky that afternoon.[19] An unverified legend, propagated mostly by himself, holds that during his one and only game of bowling, at age six, he landed three consecutive strikes throwing left-handed — and then retired from the sport immediately and permanently. The same story claims that, on the evening of his birth, a blackout hit the neighbourhood for exactly as long as it took to correctly reboot the home router.

Drugman was born and raised in Milan, where he still lives with his parents and a Linux partition he swears is "basically stable by now".[4] Sources agree that he achieved full self-awareness the first time a training run finished successfully overnight, and has been chasing that feeling ever since.

As a teenager he spent a year on exchange in the United States, an experience he describes as "formative" and of which, curiously, he mostly remembers the time zone.[dubious – discuss]

Education

He earns a bachelor's degree in Artificial Intelligence, an inter-university programme spanning three public universities — Pavia, Milano Statale and Bicocca — established in the very year he enrolled: at the time of registration the programme, quite simply, did not yet exist, making Drugman as much a first-cohort guinea pig as a student.[1] His thesis, developed during an internship at a semiconductor company, concerned hyperparameter optimisation for embedded systems — a topic he was, until the internship began, largely unaware of.

He continues on to a master's in Computer Science and Engineering at Politecnico di Milano, where he deliberately chose a project-based track over an exam-heavy one, later describing it as "slower, but at least I suffer with a purpose".

Exchange in Shenzhen

In 2025 he flies to Shenzhen for an exchange semester at CUHK, an experience he describes as "surprisingly well documented photographically" and which, according to some observers, contributed decisively to this article's "Miracles" section.[5] Shenzhen, it should be noted, went from fishing village to a metropolis of over 17 million people and the tech heart of Guangdong in roughly four decades[18] — a growth rate his academic life has tried, with mixed success, to keep pace with. During his stay he kept running research experiments on his dual-RTX server back in Milan, remotely, proving that the one thing more stable than his dual-boot is his SSH connection.

Career [edit | edit source]

During an internship as an AI Engineer at a major semiconductor company, Drugman contributes to projects he cannot discuss due to a non-disclosure agreement — a detail which, according to colleagues, earned him a nickname never officially confirmed.[6] He has also spent years working as an industrial robotics trainer on Comau and Fanuc platforms, teaching robotic arms to perform useful and, occasionally, vaguely unsettling actions. The role regularly takes him to technical institutes and vocational schools scattered across Northern Italy, in a commute colleagues describe as "geographically ambitious".

He is also a certified museum guide, a role that requires him to explain complex technical concepts to varied audiences — a skill he exercises regularly outside working hours too, unprompted.

He was selected for an AI traineeship at the European Court of Auditors, in Luxembourg, a fact he has since cited in emails to landlords, doctors, professors, universities and — apparently — an encyclopedia.[7]

Hornofecinty [edit | edit source]

🔬 The neutrality of this section is disputed, mainly because no one else can define this word. Discussion ongoing on the talk page.

Hornofecinty (from Drugmanian hornofek, "to over-engineer beyond necessity", + -inty, a suffix of uncertain meaning) is a proposed field of study, a personality trait and, according to its only known practitioner, "a full-on vibe".[8] It broadly describes the compulsion to solve a fifteen-minute problem with a six-week pipeline, three quantisation formats and a benchmark.

The typical approach unfolds in three phases: automate a process that would take two minutes by hand, carefully avoid any paid solution, and finally discover, weeks later, that he has reinvented — worse — a tool that already existed, free and optimised, for years. A frequently cited case: buying a Google Pixel for the sole purpose of getting unlimited Google Photos storage, a perk reserved at the time for that phone line, followed by building an automated backup pipeline he himself judged "frankly janky", later abandoned out of sheer exhaustion and, in his words, "to be picked back up eventually".

Acute symptoms include: opening a terminal for a task that would take one double-click; checking a training run's metrics at 3 a.m. "just for a second"; describing a successful party in terms of uptime. The condition is chronic, incurable and, according to most accounts, structurally indistinguishable from his personality.[data unconfirmed]

Miracles [edit | edit source]

Several prodigies are attributed to Drugman, none confirmed by the competent commission:

  • getting remote access working on the first try, with no authentication errors (not reproducible);
  • convincing a cloud file-sync service to behave identically on Linux and Windows;
  • surviving an entire semester in Shenzhen without losing the power adapter;
  • asking a question in class and receiving an understandable answer on the first try;[10]
  • learning, across an entire semester in Shenzhen, exactly six words of Chinese — two of which, apparently, were insults.

He is also credited with the ability to give a talk in a language he is still learning, standing up, in front of a patient audience, without (entirely) losing his train of thought.[11]

Controversies [edit | edit source]

Drugman has been involved in several disputes, nearly all with himself:

  • In a little-reported incident, he cited a percentage improvement without immediately stating the underlying absolute value. He later apologised, added the missing number and now includes it pre-emptively even in casual conversation.[12]
  • He has admitted, under mild social pressure, to having named his two GPUs — names he then refused to disclose.
  • He has not written an email without AI assistance in at least three years, a detail he considers efficiency and recipients consider differently.[13]
  • He maintains a contentious relationship with Peroni beer, which he holds — with data never published nor, frankly, requested — to be the best value-for-money beer available at the supermarket, defended at every social occasion as "underrated" with an enthusiasm some attendees consider clinically significant.
  • He is known for systematically, generously offering up his apartment for impromptu parties and aperitivo gatherings — a practice the building's condominium has yet to formally contest, for lack of documented evidence.
  • He publicly holds that coffee is overrated and that tiramisù is, organoleptically speaking, the most basic dessert in existence — positions that have gotten him informally ejected from at least two bar conversations.

Despite repeated requests, Drugman has never confirmed nor denied whether Docker Compose is "fundamentally DevOps", citing an ongoing internal investigation.

Personal life [edit | edit source]

Drugman lives in Milan with his parents and a to-do list under constant, ambitious revision.[14] His interests include terminals, dual-booting and the tendency to tell people the local model is "honestly pretty close" to the best commercial ones, then quietly adding "on some tasks". He is also described as chronically incapable of turning down a chance to organise something, see people or propose a last-minute plan of questionable wisdom, usually accompanied by a drink.

He is fluent in Italian, English and passive-aggressive commit messages. Contrary to popular belief, he does not live inside a microcontroller, though he has considered it for the low latency.

Death [edit | edit source]

⚠️ This section is written as a prophecy. Help improve it by waiting.

Drugman is, as of this writing, alive and behind on a deadline. This section exists anyway because he insisted the article be "complete".[15]

According to a plan he has left in writing — on which family members decline to comment — four vaguely threatening figures, dressed in black with sunglasses, are expected to show up at the funeral, position themselves silently in front of the casket, leave a playing card on it, deliver a curt "We'll miss you, boss" and vanish immediately afterward, never to be seen again.

According to the most widely held projection, he will not so much die as stop running — his consciousness, by then quantised to INT8 and loaded onto a microcontroller that draws a few milliwatts and never sleeps. Historians agree his last words will probably be "let me just double-check one more time", and that, tragically, the check will pass.[16] A commemorative README.md is planned.
